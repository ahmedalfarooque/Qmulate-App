# Problem Statement

How the Nazir mandate breaks under spreadsheets — and what the platform must replace.

Status: Draft v0.1 · Privileged & Confidential

---

## The role is regulated, deadline-driven, and personally liable

QMULATE acts as professional **Nazir** (trustee) for family **waqf** endowments under the Saudi **General Authority for Awqaf**, governed by the Awqaf Law (Royal Decree M/11), the Nazarah regulation, and the 2025 Beneficial-Ownership Standards (see [`../../domain/regulations/`](../../domain/regulations/) and [02 · Business context](../brd/02-business-context.md)). This is not administrative work with soft deadlines — it is a fiduciary mandate with hard, clock-driven statutory obligations measured in **KSA business days** (Sun–Thu, Hijri-moving holidays) on a **dual Hijri/Gregorian** calendar: **register within 30 business days**, **update within 15**, **notify asset substitution (istibdal) within 10**, **distribute within 3 months** of fiscal-year-end when the *Shart al-Waqif* sets no schedule, and **retain official records in Arabic for ≥10 years**. In parallel it demands continuous controls that never "close": **no commingling** of waqf funds (dedicated bank accounts per endowment), **continuous UBO/KYC verification** of every beneficiary, **immediate AML suspicion reporting with no tipping-off**, and **classification-specific SOCPA-audited statements, budgets, and beneficiary reporting** for large and medium waqfs (small waqfs file a simplified annual statement). The waqif's stipulated conditions — the generational **tier (ṭabaqa)** and **line (ẓuhūr/buṭūn)** entitlement order — are legally binding and cannot be deviated from without Authority approval. Failure is not a rounding error: penalties escalate to **dismissal of the Nazir** and fines up to **SAR 500,000**, and QMULATE's authorized representatives are **jointly and personally liable**.

## Today's reality — SharePoint and spreadsheets — cannot carry it

The first secured engagement is one family with **four endowments across three waqifs**, administered the way most family waqfs are: SharePoint folders, documents, and manual tracking. That approach fails on every dimension the mandate is judged on. It **cannot compute deadlines** — nothing converts a fiscal-year-end or a deed-registration event into a business-day countdown on the Hijri/Gregorian calendar, so statutory windows are missed silently rather than surfaced. It **cannot enforce segregation of duties** — a spreadsheet has no maker/checker gate on money movements or regulator filings, so the same person can originate and approve a distribution. It **cannot enforce the generational distribution rules** — the ordered ("al-aʿlā fa-l-aʿlā") versus shared ("tashrik") entitlement resolution, the maintenance (ṣiyāna) and Nazir-fee waterfall, and the KYC/UBO gates on each payout line are all left to human memory and hand arithmetic on money that must be tracked to the halala. It **cannot isolate UBO and AML data** — sensitive beneficial-ownership records and no-tipping-off SAR facts sit in the same shared folders as routine files, with no per-endowment access matrix and no beneficiary self-isolation. It **cannot survive an audit or a dispute** — there is no immutable, append-only record of who did what, when, and what the values were before and after, so a challenge from the Authority, an auditor, or a beneficiary cannot be answered with evidence. And it **cannot scale past one client** — every new endowment multiplies manual coordination, so onboarding is a matter of months, not days.

## What the platform must therefore do

The platform's job is to **operationalize the Nazir mandate end-to-end** and turn each of the failures above into an enforced, auditable system control (see [01 · Executive summary](../brd/01-executive-summary.md) and [05 · Operating model](../brd/05-operating-model.md)). A **deadline/compliance engine** must compute every statutory window in KSA business days on both calendars and refuse to let them lapse unseen. A **distribution engine** must apply the maintenance-first, Nazir-fee waterfall and the tier/line entitlement resolver deterministically, gated by beneficiary KYC/UBO status. **Segregation of duties** (maker ≠ checker) must be structural on every money movement and filing, dedicated per-endowment bank accounts must prevent commingling, and official financial records must be kept in Arabic. A **per-endowment access matrix** with beneficiary self-isolation must protect UBO and AML data, and an **immutable append-only audit trail** must make every material action, approval, and access reconstructable for an audit or dispute. Getting these controls right is what lets QMULATE hold its **five zero-tolerance KPIs** — on-time Authority filings, no commingling, current beneficiary KYC, no missed AML reports, no unlicensed regulated real-estate activity — and onboard the next family in days rather than months.
